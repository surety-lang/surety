more_itertools.recipes.dotproduct (from itertools recipes (more_itertools.recipes) 11.2.1)

Signature, in words: `dotproduct` takes the required arguments `vec1`, `vec2`.

The docstring:

Returns the dot product of the two iterables.

>>> dotproduct([10, 15, 12], [0.65, 0.80, 1.25])
33.5
>>> 10 * 0.65 + 15 * 0.80 + 12 * 1.25
33.5

In Python 3.12 and later, use ``math.sumprod()`` instead.
